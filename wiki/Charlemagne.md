# Lines to Charlemagne

*Last revised: 2026-11-01*

## Current consensus

- **Documented descent is achievable and common.** Tens of thousands of living people hold lineage-society-verified lines to Charlemagne (c. 747–814). The route is almost always through a medieval "gateway ancestor", usually an English, Scottish, French or Low Countries noble whose own royal descent is established in scholarly reference works. **[A/B]**
- **Statistical descent is near-universal for people with European ancestry.** Chang (1999), Rohde, Olson & Chang (2004) and Ralph & Coop (2013) show that the most recent common ancestor of all Europeans lived roughly 1,000 years ago and that anyone of that era who left descendants is an ancestor of essentially all Europeans today. Charlemagne, with well-attested descendants, is the canonical example. **[A]**
- **DNA cannot confirm or deny a line to Charlemagne.** No remains attributed to him or to any close Carolingian relative have been sequenced. After about 40 generations the expected autosomal contribution from one ancestor is below detection and is usually literally zero. His Y-DNA and mtDNA haplogroups are unknown. **[A]**
- **His own ancestry is thin.** Only about eight to ten of Charlemagne's ancestors are considered proven. Earlier generations (Arnulfing, Pippinid, and proposed Merovingian or Gallo-Roman links) rest on reconstruction, most notably Christian Settipani's work. **[B]**

## Documented descent

### Gateway ancestors

The standard reference works for North American researchers are Douglas Richardson's *Royal Ancestry* (5 vols, 2013), *Plantagenet Ancestry* (2nd ed. 2011) and *Magna Carta Ancestry* (2nd ed. 2011), and Gary Boyd Roberts's *The Royal Descents of 900 Immigrants* (2018 ed.). For continental lines, the Foundation for Medieval Genealogy's online *Medieval Lands* database (Charles Cawley) is the most-cited open source and is updated continuously. **[B]**

Lineage societies that verify Charlemagne descents:

- Order of the Crown of Charlemagne in the United States of America (publishes an approved gateway list and lineage volumes) **[A for verified lines]**
- National Society Magna Charta Dames and Barons; Baronial Order of Magna Charta (most Magna Carta sureties descend from Charlemagne)
- Society of Descendants of Charlemagne (smaller, newer)

### Common pitfalls

- Trusting user-contributed trees (Geni's "Charlemagne: Direct Descent Line" project, WikiTree, FamilySearch Family Tree) without checking each generation against a primary or scholarly source. **[D]**
- Lines through "Ancient Planters" or early colonial families that were disproved in *TAG* or the *Register* decades ago but persist online.
- Confusing the *mathematical* certainty of descent with a *documented* line.

## DNA and Charlemagne

- No sequenced remains. Charlemagne's tomb in Aachen was opened several times (1000, 1165, 1215, 1861, 1988). A 1988–2014 study of the Aachen bones by Frank Rühli and colleagues measured the skeleton and concluded it was plausibly his, but did not publish genetic data. **[B]**
- Male-line extinction. All legitimate Carolingian male lines were extinct by the 11th–12th centuries, so no living man can be matched to a "Carolingian" Y haplogroup. Commercial products marketed as "Carolingian royalty" DNA matches are not evidence. **[C]**
- Adjacent ancient DNA. Maarten Larmuseau's group (KU Leuven) has published kinship studies on Merovingian and medieval cemeteries in Belgium, which bear on the social world of Charlemagne's ancestors but not on his genome. **[A]**
- Population genetics. Ralph & Coop (2013, *PLOS Biology*) used 2,257 European genomes to show that any two Europeans share many genealogical ancestors within the last 1,000 years. This is the empirical backbone of the "everyone descends from Charlemagne" claim. **[A]**

## History of the question

- 1999: Joseph Chang's model of recent common ancestry. 2004: Rohde, Olson & Chang in *Nature* set the MRCA of all living humans at a few thousand years ago.
- 2002–2004: Steve Olson's *Mapping Human History* and *Atlantic* essay popularise "descent from Charlemagne" for a general audience.
- 2013: Ralph & Coop; Adam Rutherford and National Geographic coverage.
- 2013: Richardson's *Royal Ancestry* consolidates the gateway-ancestor literature.
- 2025-01: Nathaniel Lane Taylor publishes a revised paper on the Merovingian ancestry of Charlemagne, updating a 1993 analysis and reviewing Settipani's reconstructions. **[B]**
- 2026-10: Popular posts (e.g. White Glove Genealogy, 12 Oct 2026) restate the statistical argument; no new scholarship. **[C]**

## Open questions

- Will any Carolingian-era royal or aristocratic burial be sequenced? Candidates include burials at Aachen, Saint-Denis, Prüm, Lorsch and Metz. None announced.
- Does Settipani's proposed Gallo-Roman senatorial link for the Arnulfings hold up against new charter and prosopographical work?
- Will FamilySearch, Ancestry or Geni ever flag disproven gateway lines at scale?
