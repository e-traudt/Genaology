# Lines to Julius Caesar

*Last revised: 2026-11-01*

## Current consensus

- **No documented biological descendant of Gaius Julius Caesar (100–44 BC) exists.** His only legitimate child, Julia, died in childbirth in 54 BC and her infant died with her. Caesarion, his son by Cleopatra, was executed in 30 BC at age 17 and left no known issue. **[A]**
- **The Julian name passed by adoption, not blood.** Octavian (Augustus) was Caesar's great-nephew and adopted heir. The Julio-Claudian dynasty ended with Nero in AD 68. Augustus's own descendants through Julia the Elder are sometimes loosely called "descendants of Caesar"; they are descendants of his sister Julia Minor, not of Caesar himself. **[A]**
- **No accepted "descent from antiquity" (DFA) line reaches the Julii.** DFA is a recognised research goal in medieval genealogy. The strongest candidate routes run through Armenian (Bagratuni / Arsacid), Byzantine, and late-Roman senatorial families into the Carolingian and Merovingian aristocracy. None has been accepted as proven by the scholarly community, and none connects specifically to Caesar's family. **[B]**
- **Statistical descent is a separate matter.** If any of Caesar's kin (through his sisters or the wider *gens Julia*) left descendants who survived into the medieval period, then by the same logic that applies to Charlemagne essentially everyone of Mediterranean or European ancestry would descend from them. This is a probabilistic statement about the *gens*, not a line to the man. **[B]**

## The Julii and their claimed origins

- The *gens Julia* claimed descent from Iulus (Ascanius), son of Aeneas, and so from Venus. This is dynastic myth. **[D]**
- The historical Julii Caesares are traceable to Sextus Julius Caesar, praetor 208 BC. Wilhelm Drumann's 19th-century *Geschichte Roms* remains the basis for the family's reconstruction; the generations before Caesar's grandfather are partly conjectural. **[B]**
- Caesar's closest collateral kin with surviving lines: his sister Julia Minor (grandmother of Augustus) and the descendants of Augustus's daughter Julia the Elder (through Agrippina the Elder, to Caligula, Agrippina the Younger and Nero). All these lines are extinct in the record by the late 1st century AD. **[A]**

## Descent from antiquity: the research frontier

| Route | Status | Key scholars |
|---|---|---|
| Late-Roman senatorial families → Gallo-Roman bishops → Merovingian/Carolingian nobility | Plausible in parts, unproven as a chain | Christian Settipani, *Continuité gentilice et continuité familiale dans les familles sénatoriales romaines à l'époque impériale* (2000) and *La Préhistoire des Capétiens* (1993) |
| Arsacid / Bagratuni Armenian royalty → Byzantine and Georgian royalty → Komnenoi → Western Europe | Best-documented DFA candidate, but does not reach the Julii | Settipani, Cyril Toumanoff |
| Herodian / Judean → Byzantine | Fragmentary | — |
| Direct Julio-Claudian survival | No evidence | — |

The Foundation for Medieval Genealogy's journal *Foundations* and the soc.genealogy.medieval archive are the places where DFA proposals are debated. **[B]**

## Roman-era ancient DNA

Ancient DNA now gives a population-level picture of Imperial Rome but has not identified any member of the Julian family.

- Antonio et al., *Science* 2019: 127 genomes from Rome and Latium. Imperial-era Romans show a strong shift toward Eastern Mediterranean and Near Eastern ancestry. **[A]**
- Posth et al., *Science Advances* 2021 (Etruscans and Imperial Latium) and later syntheses (c. 250 genomes across Italy, Iberia, the Balkans, Anatolia and the Levant, 800 BCE–1400 CE) refine that picture. **[A]**
- Pilot studies on provincial Roman cemeteries (e.g. Histria, Romania, dental calculus and dentin) are extending coverage. **[A]**
- No sequenced individual has been proposed as a Julio-Claudian. The imperial mausoleums (Mausoleum of Augustus) were emptied in antiquity and the Middle Ages.

## How commercial trees handle Caesar

FamilySearch, Geni and WikiTree all carry profiles for Caesar (e.g. FamilySearch L6BJ-ZDN). Any "descendant" link from such profiles to a living person is user-generated and should be graded **[D]**. Most pass through fabricated medieval pedigrees (e.g. the "Trojan descent" pedigrees of Frankish and British kings).

## History of the question

- Antiquity–Renaissance: Trojan-origin myths give Frankish, British and Italian dynasties a notional Julian/Aenean ancestry.
- 19th c.: Drumann reconstructs the Julii from sources.
- 1980s–2000s: Settipani's DFA programme sets the modern methodological standard.
- 2019–2021: Roman ancient DNA studies published; none touch the Julii.
- 2026: No developments.

## Open questions

- Will any securely identified Julio-Claudian or Julian-gens burial ever be found and sequenced? Prospects are poor.
- Will a DFA line through the Gallo-Roman senatorial aristocracy be accepted? Watch *Foundations* and Settipani's updates.
