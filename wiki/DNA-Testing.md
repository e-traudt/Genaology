## Themes to track

### 1. Whole-genome sequencing enters the mainstream
MyHeritage DNA 360° at 30x coverage changes what a "genealogy test" produces: full Y and mitochondrial sequences, rare-variant health data, and raw data that can be uploaded to YFull or analysed with third-party tools. Expect FamilyTreeDNA and Ancestry responses in 2027. Watch for match-quality implications (WGS vs array imputation) and pricing. **[B]**

### 2. Ethnicity estimate churn
Ancestry's October 2026 update is the largest in several years. Users report big swings between versions. The lesson for genealogists is unchanged: ethnicity estimates are population-genetics summaries with confidence intervals, not records. "Regions by Grandparent" is an inference and should be treated as a hint. **[C]**

### 3. Y-DNA tree growth and ancient-DNA integration
The FTDNA haplotree crossing 100,000 branches, together with YFull and ancient-sample databases (AADR, FTDNA Discover's ancient connections), means many testers can now place a medieval or ancient sample on their own line. This is the main tool for the Genghis Khan question (see [Genghis-Khan.md](Genghis-Khan.md)) and would be for Charlemagne if a Carolingian sample ever existed. **[B]**

### 4. Ownership, privacy and the 23andMe aftermath
23andMe's sale to TTAM (court-approved 30 June 2025, $305 million) set a template: state attorneys general extracted deletion rights, research opt-out, and a privacy advisory board. 2026 items to watch: TTAM's first annual AG report, any law-enforcement access requests, and state genetic-privacy laws modelled on Montana's and Texas's. **[B]**

### 5. Forensic / investigative genetic genealogy (IGG)
IGG continues to resolve cold cases weekly. Example from the latest pass: Ottawa County, Ohio, Jane Doe (1970s) under active IGG work with a private lab, reported 28 Oct 2026. Policy fronts: DOJ interim policy, Maryland and Montana statutes, GEDmatch PRO and FTDNA opt-in rules, and the growing use of WGS on degraded samples. **[C]**

### 6. Ancient DNA as a genealogical source
Population-scale ancient DNA (Roman Italy, Golden Horde elites, medieval Belgian cemeteries) is now routinely used to test dynastic and "descent" claims. The 2026 PNAS Golden Horde paper is the model: named mausoleum, tradition of identity, Y-haplotype, kinship among burials. **[A]**

## Practical guidance (stable)

- Deep paternal line: FamilyTreeDNA Big Y-700, or WGS (MyHeritage DNA 360°, Nebula) then upload to YFull.
- Deep maternal line: full mtDNA sequence (FTDNA) or WGS.
- Cousin matching: Ancestry first (database size), then transfer raw data to MyHeritage, FTDNA, GEDmatch, LivingDNA.
- Medieval royal descent: DNA cannot verify it. Use DNA to confirm the first 5–7 generations of paper trail, then documents.
- Always download raw data and keep it off-platform.

## History of the question

- 2000: FamilyTreeDNA founded; Y-STR testing begins.
- 2007: 23andMe launches consumer autosomal testing.
- 2012: AncestryDNA launches.
- 2018: Golden State Killer case popularises IGG.
- 2023–2024: 23andMe data breach; bankruptcy filing March 2025; TTAM acquisition July 2025.
- 2026: Ancestry Origins update (Oct); MyHeritage DNA 360° (WGS); FTDNA 100k branches; 23andMe ASHG health-results analysis (Oct).
