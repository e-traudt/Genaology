# Lines to Genghis Khan

*Last revised: 2026-11-01*

## Current consensus

- **Documented descent exists but is narrower than the genetic claim.** The Chinggisid (Borjigin) lineage is one of the best-attested royal pedigrees in Asia. Documented descendants include the Mongol, Yuan, Golden Horde, Chagatai, Ilkhanate, Timurid (by marriage and claimed descent), Mughal, Kazakh, Crimean, Kazan and Bukharan ruling houses, and Mongolian and Inner Mongolian noble families recorded in Qing-era registers. Many living families can document lines through these. **[A/B]**
- **The 2003 "1 in 200 men" claim is now doubtful.** Zerjal et al. (2003) found a Y-chromosome star cluster (then called C3*, now C2b1a3a1-F3796 / C2*-ST) carried by about 8% of men across 16 Asian populations and 0.5% of men worldwide, and attributed it to Genghis Khan and his male relatives. The attribution was inference, not a match to any Chinggisid remains. **[A for the cluster, B for the attribution]**
- **Ancient DNA has produced three different candidate haplogroups for Chinggisid males**, which cannot all be right and may reflect misattributed burials or non-paternity:
  - **R1b-M343** from elite burials at Tavan Tolgoi, Sukhbaatar, eastern Mongolia (Lkhagvasuren et al. 2016), proposed as the Golden Family. **[A]**
  - **Q** from a Yuan-period Mongol noble family mausoleum in Hebei, northern China. **[A]**
  - **C3* (rare sub-branch)** from three paternally related men in Golden Horde elite mausoleums at Ulytau, Kazakhstan, including the mausoleum locally attributed to Jochi, Genghis Khan's eldest son (PNAS, February 2026). **[A]**
- **The Ulytau result cuts both ways.** It is the first ancient-DNA finding of C3* in a burial with a Chinggisid tradition, which supports the broad hypothesis. But the specific branch is very rare in living populations, so the widespread modern star cluster cannot be explained by descent from these men alone. The identification of the Jochi burial itself rests on local tradition and is not confirmed by the paper. **[A]**

## The star-cluster claim

| Year | Finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| 2003 | Zerjal et al., *AJHG*: star cluster in ~16 million men; attributed to Genghis Khan. | A / B |
| 2005–2007 | Further star clusters identified (Giocangga/Qing, Nurhaci lineage; others), showing the pattern is not unique. | A |
| 2015 | Balaresque et al., *EJHG*: 11 Asian founding lineages, several unrelated to the Mongols. | A |
| 2016 | Lkhagvasuren et al., *PLOS ONE*: Tavan Tolgoi elite burials carry R1b, not C3*. | A |
| 2018 | Wei et al., *J Hum Genet*: argues the star cluster C2b1a3a1-F3796 originated c. 2,600 years ago among proto-Mongolic / Niru'un peoples, pre-dating Genghis Khan; proposes C2c1a1a1-M407 (found in Dayan Khan descendants) as the better Chinggisid candidate. | A |
| 2019 | Lu clan study (NW China, claimed descent from Toghan, 6th son): mostly C2b1a1b1-F1756, a third candidate. | A |
| 2025-06 | bioRxiv preprint: Golden Horde elite genomes (Ulytau). | B |
| 2026-02 | PNAS publication of the same: C3* rare branch in three related males; "fewer people related to Genghis Khan than thought" framing in press. | A |

**Net effect:** As of 2026 there is no single Y haplogroup that can be called "Genghis Khan's" with confidence. A living man carrying the common star cluster cannot be said to descend from him on that basis.

## Documented descent (Chinggisids)

- Primary sources: *Secret History of the Mongols*, Rashid al-Din's *Jami' al-tawarikh*, *Yuan Shi*, Qing *Iledkel shastir* genealogies, Kazakh *shezhire*, Crimean and Kazan khanate records.
- Living documented lines: Mongolian Borjigin families (post-1990 revival of clan names), Kazakh *töre* (white bone) lineages, Inner Mongolian banner nobility, descendants of Crimean Giray and Kazan khans in Russia and Turkey, Mughal descendants in South Asia.
- Western lines: a small number of European noble families descend from Tatar princes (e.g. Russian princely houses of Chinggisid origin such as Yusupov, Urusov, Kudashev by tradition). Each needs generation-by-generation checking. **[B/D]**
- No accepted documented line connects Genghis Khan to Western European royalty, despite popular claims.

## Ancient DNA and the search for the tomb

- The February 2026 PNAS Golden Horde paper also shows the Ulytau elites had predominantly Mongolian ancestry with Central Eurasian admixture, consistent with a ruling class that remained genetically distinct from local populations for generations. **[A]**
- Press coverage (March 2026) framed the result as "bringing researchers closer to Genghis Khan's tomb" by giving a Y-haplotype to test against any candidate burial. No candidate burial has been announced; the Ikh Khorig / Burkhan Khaldun area remains protected. **[C]**

## Open questions

- Which of R1b, Q, C2-F3796, C2-M407, C2-F1756 or the Ulytau C3* branch, if any, is the true Borjigin paternal line? Resolving this needs a securely identified burial of a Chinggisid with an unbroken documented link (e.g. a Yuan emperor, a named Golden Horde khan).
- Are the Tavan Tolgoi burials Borjigin or an allied (Ongud / Khongirad) lineage?
- Will the Ulytau team publish higher-coverage Y data allowing placement on the YFull / FTDNA trees so living testers can compare?
